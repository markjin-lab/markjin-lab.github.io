## Today’s news through the Unified Systems View

**Item 1: At ten-million-token context, can sparse attention still keep accuracy while cutting one-layer prefill and decode by another order of magnitude?**

Background: Agentic workflows are shifting toward long-horizon tasks where models benefit from longer context windows, but at ultra-long context (above 1M tokens in the abstract) full attention is prohibitively expensive. Sparse attention uses an indexer to pick a subset of keys, yet current indexing either still has quadratic cost in context size or loses accuracy at ultra-long scale [1]. (General explanation added by this site: prefill usually means the stage that processes the full input context before generation; decode usually means the later stage that emits tokens step by step; sparse attention usually means computing attention only over a selected subset of query–key pairs rather than all of them.)

In a paper posted on arXiv on 2026-10-30, "Latent Sparse Attention: Towards Subquadratic Ultra-Long Context Attention", ten authors—Ales Kubicek, Andrea Caciolai, Liangzu Peng, Sukrit Sumant, Benjamin Hoover, Fraser Greenlee, Ruairidh M. Battleday, Radim Urban, Torsten Hoefler and James C. R. Whittington—introduce Latent Sparse Attention (LSA), which hierarchically clusters keys in a learned latent space so queries score the cluster hierarchy instead of every key, speeding prefill, decode and training at long context; they give two variants, Distilled-LSA (D-LSA) that can be applied to pretrained models via distillation, and Native-LSA (N-LSA) trained natively on the next-token loss alone [1].

Why it matters: on GLM-5.3-FLASH (QWEN-3.8-27B) at 10M tokens, D-LSA speeds up one attention layer by 22.7× (72.4×) in prefill and 4.6× (42.5×) in decode over its original sparse attention (full attention), while keeping similar accuracy across a variety of tasks [1]. N-LSA matches the accuracy of full attention with a training speedup of 1.6× per layer at 128k tokens [1].

**Item 2: At extreme sparsity, does high-quality pruning still require model-sized optimizer state?**

Background: Weight pruning can cut LLM storage and inference cost, but preserving quality at high sparsity remains hard. One-shot pruning is cheap yet degrades sharply as sparsity rises; another line optimizes the mask from the dense model under the end-to-end objective, and a recent extreme-sparsity method reaches high quality only with model-sized optimizer state and auxiliary variables [2]. (General explanation added by this site: sparsity usually means the share of weights set to zero or removed; perplexity usually means a language-model score of how hard text is to predict, with lower usually better.)

In a paper posted on arXiv on 2026-10-30, "Prune Gradually, Adapt Sparsely: Efficient Pruning of Large Language Models at Extreme Sparsity", seven authors—Jean Lucien Randrianantenaina, Hung Nguyen, Nasib Ullah, Jinbin Zhang, Nilesh Gupta, Inderjit S. Dhillon and Rohit Babbar—propose ETAPS, which tightens a global sparsity constraint in progressive stages while training a sparse delta under a small fixed budget; they argue that gradual weight removal keeps each step small enough for sparse adaptation to compensate, so end-to-end gradients from the adapted model can guide the next prune without model-sized optimizer state [2].

Why it matters: across six models from 125M to 13B parameters, ETAPS achieves lower perplexity than ELSA at every evaluated sparsity from 50% to 90%, with perplexity reductions of up to 42% [2]. It is also far cheaper: at 90% sparsity, it prunes LLaMA2-7B on a single H200, where ELSA uses four, with $5.0\times$ smaller total persistent state and 49% fewer GPU-hours [2].

**Item 3: When self-distillation already keeps an EMA teacher trajectory, does Muon still need a separate momentum buffer?**

Background: Self-distillation methods for LLMs with an exponential moving average (EMA) teacher already retain a history of student updates, yet Muon still stores a separate momentum buffer and raises persistent training memory [3]. (General explanation added by this site: an EMA teacher usually means a teacher whose weights are an exponential moving average of the student; persistent training memory usually means optimizer state and related buffers that occupy device memory beyond the model weights during training.)

In a paper posted on arXiv on 2026-10-30, "MUON-RT Reusing the Teacher's Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation" (entry states Under Submission), three authors—Aryan Das, Vinay Kumar Verma and Aakansha Mishra—propose Muon with Reused Teacher (Muon-RT), which reuses the teacher–student displacement to drop momentum buffers for hidden weight matrices; it removes the radial component, normalizes spectrally, and mixes the result with the current gradient after Newton-Schulz orthogonalization [3].

Why it matters: with Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning (SDFT) on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Muon-RT improves average accuracy over AdamW by 2.6 points with 42% less persistent memory, and over momentum Muon by 5.4 points with 27% less persistent memory [3]. With Self-Distillation Policy Optimization (SDPO) on Olmo-3-7B-Instruct, it improves average accuracy over AdamW by 2.0 points [3].
