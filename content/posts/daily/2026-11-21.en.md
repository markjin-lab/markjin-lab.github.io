Three research leads in this issue examine how much history customer forecasts can use, which sources news searches return, and how to estimate part distortion in metal additive manufacturing. The arXiv metadata records all three preprints as submitted on November 19, 2026 (UTC). This brief draws on their abstracts and metadata [1], [2], [3].

## How much does extra history help customer forecasts?

Tara Sowrirajan and Yanqiao Wang study how many weeks of history a model can see before the starting point of a long-horizon customer forecast. When the comparison used the same customer-weeks across windows, the abstract reports larger gains from longer windows than in the unmatched comparison for every model family covered. The effect went in the same direction on five public datasets for both ridge regression and the authors’ Transformer [1].

On the internal data, longer windows also changed which customers could enter the training set. All test anchors fell within the same eight-week band, so the study could not isolate the effect of history length from calendar coverage. The authors’ only fully blind registered prediction also failed. An activity-mixture model predicted a 0.014 gain in log-space R² from extending the window from 24 to 32 weeks. A weekly-aggregate probe found a decline of 0.0004. A further check would use forecast starting points in different seasons and hold the customer cohort fixed [1].

## How much news comes from domestic sources in Latin American search?

Francisco W. Kerche and colleagues built queries around 1,901 current events in nine Latin American countries. They submitted the queries to three search engines and four language models, varying the query language and user location to represent different user scenarios. The authors report that language models returned less diverse news sources in these queries. For local users, 41.3% of the news returned by search engines was domestic, and the share returned by language models was 3.6 to 11.6 percentage points lower [2].

The pattern for domestic news varied across user types. The abstract does not provide enough detail to recalculate the differences country by country. A follow-up check would examine the number of queries per country and scenario, the rules for classifying a source as domestic, and the treatment of syndicated stories [2].

## Predicting powder bed fusion distortion from part geometry

Saima Ahmed Suhi and colleagues study how to predict surface displacement before fabricating parts by laser powder bed fusion. Their model uses both the part’s surface mesh and descriptors of its overall geometry, with simulated displacement from ANSYS Additive Print as the reference. In five-fold cross-validation split by part across 117 parts, the model achieved an R² of 0.946 and a mean absolute error of 0.0178 mm for displacement magnitude. A node-wise multilayer perceptron achieved an R² of 0.913 on the same task [3].

Removing the mass-distribution descriptors lowered the R² for displacement magnitude to 0.900. These results measure agreement with simulated values. A further check would compare predictions with independently measured displacement under different geometries and manufacturing conditions [3].
