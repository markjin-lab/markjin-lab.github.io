Visual Token Routing (VTR) lets agents that operate computers process fewer pieces of screenshots; after 2,000 training steps, it matches the full-resolution model on OSWorld and other tests with 2.5–5.8× fewer image units, cutting inference cost by up to 3.3× and speeding up pre-answer input processing by up to 2.9× [1]. Another training-free method for long-context decoding reports up to 1.26× / 2.22× faster decoding at 64k / 256k tokens on Qwen3.5-4B [3].

## Today’s news through the Unified Systems View

For what the Unified Systems View is, see the [About page](/en/about/). All three papers in this issue can be read on arXiv; none of the three arXiv entries states publication or review status [1], [2], [3].

**Item 1: Can computer-use agents cut inference cost by keeping far fewer visual tokens while staying close to full-resolution task performance?**

Background: Computer-use agents (CUAs) have recently reached superhuman performance on several long-horizon benchmarks, yet encoding each screenshot as thousands of visual tokens piles into long contexts over extended interaction histories and raises compute and latency. The abstract says most of these visual tokens are either semantically irrelevant to the agent’s next action or temporally redundant across screenshots [1]. (General explanation added by this site: a visual token usually means a patch-level representation of an image that is fed into a language model; prefill usually means the stage that processes the input before an answer is generated; inference cost here means the compute or latency needed to finish one inference pass.)

In a paper posted on arXiv on 2026-11-04, "Visual Token Routing: Learning to keep tokens that matter", two authors—Pranjal Aggarwal and Sean Welleck—propose Visual Token Routing (VTR), a joint-training framework that trains the vision–language model together with a lightweight spatio-temporal router that examines each image region in the current and previous screenshots and decides whether its visual token enters the language model [1].

Why it matters: with just 2,000 training steps, VTR matches the performance of the full-resolution model on OSWorld, CUA-World, and video game action prediction while using 2.5–5.8× fewer visual tokens; these savings reduce inference cost by up to 3.3× and speed up prefill by up to 2.9× [1]. VTR also outperforms lowering the image resolution and other token-dropping baselines by up to 12.8 points on OSWorld [1].

**Item 2: Can agents rewrite long interaction histories into shorter summaries much faster while still keeping the information that matters?**

Background: LLM agents use context compaction to manage growing interaction histories, but summarizing long histories with large models can introduce substantial delays, so compaction must balance information retention against latency. The abstract observes that structured summary slots can retain information when generated independently [2]. (General explanation added by this site: a slot usually means a field in a structured summary.)

In a paper posted on arXiv on 2026-11-04, "SpecCompact: Accelerating LLM Agent Context Compaction with Slot-Parallel Semantic Speculative Verification", three authors—Yulu An, Yifei Feng and Xiangyang Yin—present SpecCompact, which fine-tunes a small drafter, generates slots in parallel, lets the target model verify each proposed line and repair rejected content, and continues drafting from the updated slot prefix [2].

Why it matters: across AppWorld and τ³ benchmarks with two target models, SpecCompact achieves 2.04–5.63× compaction speedups over Hermes-style summarization; a user study within Hermes further shows an 8.68× median compaction speedup [2]. Separate downstream evaluations reveal model- and task-dependent performance trade-offs [2].

**Item 3: After hybrid LLMs replace most layers that make every new token more expensive than the last, can the remaining ones generate long answers faster without any training or weight changes?**

Background: Full-attention layers make every generated token more expensive than the previous one and slow models on long-context tasks. Hybrid models cut that cost by replacing most full-attention layers with linear-attention layers, yet the remaining full-attention layers still dominate decoding at long contexts. The abstract accelerates those layers with combined sparse attention: each layer attends to a sliding window of recent tokens, a pinned prefix, and a fixed budget of most relevant tokens, with window size and budget chosen per layer by multi-objective search [3]. (General explanation added by this site: decoding usually means generating an answer one step at a time.)

In a paper posted on arXiv on 2026-11-04, "Accelerating Long-Context Decoding in Large-Scale Hybrid LLMs Without Retraining", three authors—João Eduardo Batista, Emil Vatai and Mohamed Wahib—accelerate the remaining full-attention layers without any training or weight changes [3].

Why it matters: across six hybrid models from three families, from 4B to 397B parameters, the selected configurations preserve their baseline model’s accuracy on 21 long-context datasets; decoding 64k/256k tokens becomes up to 1.26×/2.22× faster on Qwen3.5-4B, 1.20×/2.23× on Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, and 1.16×/2.06× on Ornith-1.0-397B [3].
