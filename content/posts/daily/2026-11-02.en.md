Rimuru raises baseline throughput by up to 19.03× and cuts average TTFT by 98.1% [1]. Another paper shows that under speculative decoding at the same acceptance rate, TextSeal2 detects 18 and 22 percentage points more 200-token answers than TextSeal and textGrain, respectively [2].

## Today’s news through the Unified Systems View

For what the Unified Systems View is, see the [About page](/en/about/). All three papers in this issue can be read on arXiv; the arXiv entry for item 3 states under review at ICLR 2027, while the entries for items 1 and 2 do not state publication or review status [1], [2], [3].

**Item 1: When GPU memory is tight, can weights and attention states share one elastic pool and recover both throughput and time to first token?**

Background: Modern LLM serving is strained by large model weights and attention states on device memory; systems that provision the two regions separately leave fixed boundaries that cannot move with the workload, so heavy loads starve concurrency and throughput while light loads leave idle capacity stranded. Heterogeneous tensors with different layouts and lifetimes make sharing harder still, which motivates unified, cross-type memory management [1]. (General explanation added by this site: TTFT usually means the latency from request arrival to the first output token; MoE experts usually mean the selectively routed subnetwork weights inside a mixture-of-experts model.)

In a paper posted on arXiv on 2026-10-29, "Rimuru: Unified Memory Management for Modern LLM Serving via Elastic Expert Offloading", nine authors—Zhiyuan Fang, Dingyang Li, Jianfeng Zhong, Bin Wang, Weixi Zhu, Cunshu Ni, Zicong Hong, Wuhui Chen and Zibin Zheng—present Rimuru, which unifies heterogeneous tensors through a shared page pool and memory views, reuses capacity across types with huge-page alignment and a multi-level page allocator, and lets a page-table-aware fused MoE kernel read non-contiguous expert weights directly; a workload-aware adjuster then reallocates pages across tensor types at runtime. The system is implemented on top of vLLM [1].

Why it matters: evaluations show that Rimuru achieves up to 19.03× baseline throughput and reduces TTFT by 98.1% on average [1].

Where it sticks: the abstract reports a peak throughput multiple and an average TTFT cut, without a full score table or a dollar or GPU-hour cost gap [1].

What to watch next: this is our own point to watch, not a conclusion of the paper: whether other teams, on other serving stacks or other MoE setups, still find that a unified page pool with elastic expert offloading lifts throughput by about an order of magnitude or more and cuts average TTFT near the level reported here under memory pressure.

**Item 2: When speculative decoding replaces drafted tokens, does a watermark’s signal survive without hurting acceptance?**

Background: Distortion-free watermarks aim to leave a detectable signal without changing the model’s output distribution; speculative decoding rejects some drafted tokens and replaces them, so a watermark that breaks on replacement is hard to keep under accelerated decoding [2]. (General explanation added by this site: speculative decoding usually means a smaller model drafts several next tokens and a target model verifies them in one pass, accepting or rolling back; acceptance rate usually means the share of drafted tokens the target keeps.)

In a paper posted on arXiv on 2026-10-29, "TextSeal2: Distortion-Free LLM Watermarking with Copula Mixing and Memoryless Races", fourteen authors—Tom Sander, Pierre Fernandez, Itai Gat, Haiming Bao, Jiaxin Shi, Jason Park, Valeriu Lacatusu, Sylvestre-Alvise Rebuffi, Hongyan Chang, Hady Elsahar, Tomáš Souček, Tuan Tran, Surya Parimi and Alexandre Mourachko—introduce TextSeal2, which merges keys into a virtual key through a Gaussian copula and verifies keyed drafts under speculative decoding with a memoryless race so replaced tokens stay watermarked with the same keys and the acceptance rate is unchanged; the abstract also states that it preserves output diversity and adds no measurable inference latency [2].

Why it matters: at equal output token diversity, TextSeal2 detects 8 to 22 percentage points more 100-token answers than TextSeal at a false-positive rate of \(10^{-3}\), and is comparable to textGrain while requiring no post-logits optimization [2]. Under speculative decoding, it detects 18 and 22 percentage points more 200-token answers than TextSeal and textGrain, respectively, at the same acceptance rate [2].

Where it sticks: the abstract reports detection percentage-point gaps and unchanged acceptance, without a full score table or a dollar or GPU-hour cost gap [2].

What to watch next: this is our own point to watch, not a conclusion of the paper: whether other teams, on other model families or other speculative-decoding stacks, still find that a distortion-free watermark keeps TextSeal / textGrain-scale detection gains at unchanged acceptance with no measurable latency add-on.

**Item 3: To get finer pixel-space detail, must generative Transformers always lengthen the global attention sequence?**

Background: Pixel-space generative Transformers often use one token per image patch, so finer detail usually means more tokens and costlier global attention. The authors observe that low-frequency structure benefits from global exchange while high-frequency detail can be modeled locally, which suggests decoupling output detail from global sequence length [3]. (General explanation added by this site: FID usually means a feature-distance score of how close a generated image distribution is to a real one, with lower usually better; FLOPs usually means an estimate of floating-point operations in one forward pass.)

In a paper posted on arXiv on 2026-10-29, "Read, Allocate, Route: Role-Coupled Tokenization for Pixel-Space Generative Transformers" (entry states under review at ICLR 2027), five authors—Jiongfan Yang, Weichen Fan, Haiwen Diao, Zhongang Cai and Ziwei Liu—introduce SpecSlot, which expands each spatial anchor into slots for disjoint DCT bands, lets only the lowest-frequency slot join global attention while all slots interact locally, and adds a spatial head for a local correction, keeping the generative state, target and loss in pixel space [3].

Why it matters: on ImageNet-512, SpecSlot improves FID by 2.84 over a FLOPs-matched JiT control with 3.9% fewer FLOPs, and comes within only 0.69 FID of JiT-B/16 at 38.2% lower cost [3]. On text-to-image generation, SpecSlot surpasses JiT-B/8 on DPG-Bench by 1.06 at 40.6% lower forward cost [3].

Where it sticks: the abstract reports FID gaps and FLOPs / forward-cost percentages, without a full score table or a dollar cost gap [3].

What to watch next: this is our own point to watch, not a conclusion of the paper: whether other teams, on other pixel-space backbones or other resolutions, still find that band-wise slots that separate detail from global sequence length hold or improve FID / text-to-image scores while cutting FLOPs or forward cost.
