## Unified Systems View reading

**Item 1: A direct way to calculate the logical failure rate of the toric code.** Background: on 2026-10-06 PRX Quantum published a paper that maps the logical failure rate (the probability of still getting an error after error correction) of the toric code (an error-correcting code that uses topological structure to protect quantum information) exactly onto the 2D Ising model (a classic statistical-mechanics model of many interacting small units), obtaining a closed-form expression (a result that can be written down directly as a formula) [1]. Why it matters: this belongs to quantum error correction and addresses the question of how reliable an error-correction scheme really is; the abstract says the method reaches scales beyond what current computer simulations can handle [1].

**Item 2: A source of thermal disturbance in a silicon qubit is identified.** Background: on 2026-10-07 PRX Quantum published a paper on a hole spin in silicon (the spin carried by a "hole" left where an electron is missing, which can serve as a qubit) [2]. The paper reports that its frequency drifts when heated, that the cause is an electric effect, and that there is a magnetic-field operating point immune to this drift [2]. Why it matters: this belongs to semiconductor quantum hardware and addresses the problem that qubits are sensitive to heat and become unstable; the abstract says the result can guide the design of semiconductor qubits that are more robust to heat [2].

**Item 3: NASA seeks plans for commercial space stations.** Background: on 2026-10-09 NASA issued a news release saying it had released its final Request for Proposals (a formal document in which the government invites companies to submit plans), asking industry to submit plans for the next generation of commercial space stations [3]. Earlier steps: in March NASA published two Requests for Information, in July a draft Request for Proposals, followed by an industry day; proposals are due on December 8, 2026 [3]. Why it matters: this belongs to human spaceflight and concerns who will provide space stations in low Earth orbit once the International Space Station retires; the release says the aim is to maintain a sustained presence in low Earth orbit after the station retires [3].

**Item 4: Testing heat shields by "taking out the trash".** Background: on 2026-10-09 NASA published news saying a cargo spacecraft leaving the International Space Station carried 12 small experimental capsules to test next-generation thermal protection materials, with measurements to be collected during reentry [4]. The experiment, called KREPE-3, is the third in a series of low-cost missions [4]. Why it matters: this belongs to spacecraft thermal protection and addresses whether heat shields can withstand reentry heat for astronauts returning from the Moon and Mars; the approach uses a cargo spacecraft that was leaving anyway rather than a dedicated launch [4].

**Item 6: OpenAI disrupts two "false front" influence operations.** Background: on 2026-10-08 OpenAI published a post saying it had disrupted two AI-enabled influence operations that posed as "journalists" and a think tank to spread geopolitical messaging [5].

For items 5 and 6 (the Falcon 9 launch and the OpenAI influence operations) and the items in the Signals from X section: too little information yet; no framework reading for now.
