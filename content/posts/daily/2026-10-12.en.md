**Item 1: A direct way to calculate the logical failure rate of the toric code.** Background: on 2026-10-06 PRX Quantum published a paper. Using an exact mapping to the 2D Ising model (a classic statistical-mechanics model of many interacting small units), the paper obtains closed-form logical failure rates for the toric code [1]. The toric code is an error-correcting code that uses topological structure to protect quantum information; the logical failure rate is the probability of still getting an error after error correction; and closed-form means a formula written using a finite combination of standard operations or functions. Why it matters: the paper gives closed-form logical failure rates for the toric code and says this offers an analytic tool kit for benchmarking topological error-correcting codes beyond current computational limits [1].

**Item 2: An electric origin found for thermal frequency shifts in a silicon hole spin.** Background: on 2026-10-07 PRX Quantum published a paper on a hole spin in silicon (the spin carried by a "hole" left where an electron is missing, which can serve as a qubit) [2]. The paper reports that its thermal frequency shifts come from an electric effect and identifies a magnetic-field operating point immune to them [2]. Why it matters: the paper finds an electric origin for the thermal frequency shifts and a magnetic-field operating point immune to them; the abstract says this can guide the design of thermally robust semiconductor qubits [2].

**Item 3: NASA seeks plans for commercial space stations.** Background: on 2026-10-09 NASA issued a news release saying it had released its final Request for Proposals (a formal document in which the government invites companies to submit plans), asking industry to submit plans for the next generation of commercial space stations, with the aim of maintaining a sustained presence in low Earth orbit after the International Space Station retires [3]. Why it matters: NASA says it will still need a place in low Earth orbit for research, technology development and crew training after the ISS retires; commercial space stations may provide that capability [3]. What changed: NASA has moved from requests for input and a draft to a formal solicitation; the next milestone is the December 8 proposal deadline, and NASA expects to award contracts in spring 2027 [3].

**Item 4: Testing heat shields by "taking out the trash".** Background: on 2026-10-09 NASA published news saying a cargo spacecraft leaving the International Space Station carried 12 small experimental capsules to test next-generation thermal protection materials [4]. The experiment, called KREPE-3, is the third in a series of low-cost missions [4]. Why it matters: NASA says the departing cargo spacecraft provides a way to get flight data on heat-shield materials still in development [4]. What changed: the 12 capsules were activated before departure and left with the spacecraft; the next thing to watch is whether they collect data during reentry and transmit it to researchers by satellite [4].

**Item 6: OpenAI disrupts two "false front" influence operations.** Background: on 2026-10-08 OpenAI said in its official RSS feed that it had disrupted two AI-enabled influence operations that used false-front "journalists" and a think tank to spread geopolitical messaging [5].
