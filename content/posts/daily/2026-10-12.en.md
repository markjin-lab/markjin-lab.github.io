## Unified Systems View reading

What several main items in this issue have in common is that they make something that used to be hard to calculate, measure or trace directly checkable: quantum error-correction failure rates now have a formula [1], heat-shield materials are being tested for real during a cargo spacecraft's reentry [4], and OpenAI has published its rating of the influence operations it disrupted [5].

**Item 1: a way to "just calculate" topological quantum error correction.** Background: on 2026-10-06 PRX Quantum published a paper that maps the logical failure rate (the probability of still getting an error after error correction) of the toric code (an error-correcting code that uses topological structure to protect quantum information) exactly onto the 2D Ising model (a classic statistical-mechanics model of many interacting small units), obtaining a closed-form expression (a result that can be written down directly as a formula) [1]. What changed: the abstract says this provides analytic tools for benchmarking topological codes beyond current computational limits, that is, without relying entirely on large-scale numerical simulation [1]. Worth watching: whether this method is used to benchmark other topological codes.

**Item 2: the source of thermal drift in silicon qubits has been found.** Background: on 2026-10-07 PRX Quantum published a paper on a hole spin in silicon (the spin carried by a "missing electron" position in a semiconductor, which can serve as a qubit) [2]. Where it is stuck: heat makes the frequency of this kind of qubit drift [2]. What changed: the paper reports that the drift comes from an electric effect and identifies a magnetic-field operating point immune to it; the abstract says this can guide the design of more heat-tolerant semiconductor qubits [2]. Worth watching: whether follow-up experiments are published that run qubits at this operating point.

**Item 3: NASA looks for a successor in low Earth orbit after the space station retires.** Background: on 2026-10-09 NASA issued a news release saying it had released its final Request for Proposals (a formal document in which the government invites companies to submit plans), asking industry to submit plans for the next generation of commercial space stations [3]. Where it is stuck: the reason the release gives is to maintain a sustained presence in low Earth orbit after the International Space Station retires [3]. Related steps: in March this year NASA published two Requests for Information, in July a draft Request for Proposals, followed by an industry day [3]. Worth watching: whether NASA later announces a submission deadline or the selected companies.

**Item 4: testing heat shields by "taking out the trash".** Background: on 2026-10-09 NASA published news saying a cargo spacecraft leaving the International Space Station carried 12 small experimental capsules to test next-generation thermal protection materials, with the aim of developing heat shields for astronauts returning from the Moon and Mars [4]. What changed: the experiment, called KREPE-3, uses the final moments of the cargo spacecraft's lifespan to gather data and is the third in a series of low-cost missions [4]. Worth watching: whether the measurements from these 12 capsules are published.

**Item 5: OpenAI disrupts two "false front" influence operations.** Background: on 2026-10-08 OpenAI published a post saying it had banned two covert influence operations, one from Russia and one from Iran; they combined its models with traditional techniques and used invented "journalist" personas and a "think tank" to spread geopolitical messaging to target audiences [5]. What changed: using the IO Breakout Scale (a scale that scores influence operations from 1 to 6 by how far they spread), OpenAI rates the Russian operation as Category 5 and says it is the first Category 5 operation it has disrupted since it began publishing such reports [5]. Related trend: OpenAI says that, among the 30 covert influence operations it has exposed over the past two and a half years, those that tried to place content in real media outlets had the highest potential reach [5]. Worth watching: whether later OpenAI reports include another Category 5 operation.
