## 01 The latest event: look beyond revenue scale

Earlier issues separated freight rates and industry structure. Today we go one step deeper into company accounts: how much of the contract amount must be paid to suppliers? Sinotrans’s Q3 report carries a 28 October publication date on CNINFO and 27 October on its website. We use this latest financial disclosure: by the company website’s date it is within 72 hours, not same-day operating data. The unaudited report covers January–September.

Consolidated revenue was RMB 71.987 billion, down 4.07% year on year; cost of revenue was RMB 67.448 billion. Subtracting the unrounded amounts gives a gross-profit difference of about RMB 4.538 billion, versus RMB 4.286 billion a year earlier. This is our calculation of revenue less cost of revenue, not operating profit or net profit. [1][2]

## 02 A comparable trend: a cumulative decline is not a decline every quarter

Consolidated revenue fell 8.06% in the first half and 4.07% in January–September; the report directly gives third-quarter revenue growth of 4.16%. These are the same type of indicator over different-length windows. Subtracting two cumulative growth rates does not produce quarterly growth. These monetary figures are not adjusted for price changes. [1][3]

## 03 One mechanism: purchased services are the other side of revenue

Consider a hypothetical logistics contract recognized on a gross basis. Initially the customer pays RMB 100 and purchased transport services cost RMB 90, leaving gross profit of RMB 10. Later the customer pays RMB 95 and purchasing costs RMB 83, leaving RMB 12. Revenue falls while the difference rises. These numbers illustrate a mechanism; they are not Sinotrans’s prices, cost structure or an explanation of its pricing.

As with an engineering contract, the contract price is not what remains. But not every forwarding transaction should be understood on a gross basis. Accounting asks whether the business controls the service before transferring it: a principal recognizes the gross amount, while an agent recognizes a commission or fee. Calling a business “agency” does not automatically determine its accounting role, nor may it arbitrarily change methods to inflate revenue. [4][6]

## 04 Physical activity and historical explanations: volumes cannot establish the cause

On 26 October, the Ministry of Transport reported January–September port container throughput of 280 million TEU, up 4.5%. Sinotrans’s ocean-forwarding volume was 12.141 million TEU, compared with 11.793 million a year earlier. Port handling and forwarding measure different activities: these figures cannot establish market share, and dividing group revenue by one business’s container volume does not give an average freight rate. [1][5]

The interim report offers historical context: during the first half the company withdrew from certain cross-border e-commerce logistics and logistics e-commerce platform businesses. The e-commerce segment’s external revenue fell 68.16%, and segment profit fell 14.53%. Here segment profit means segment operating profit excluding investment income, not profit attributable to shareholders. This illustrates a change in business mix, not proof of a general demand collapse. However, the first-half explanation is not a complete attribution of the nine-month revenue change. [4]

## 05 Counterevidence, feedback and what to watch next

A wider gross-profit difference does not mean “all profits grew”: January–September profit attributable to shareholders fell 0.12%, while attributable profit excluding non-recurring items increased 21.18%. Period expenses, investment income and taxes also affect the eventual result. Excluding non-recurring items does not turn profit into operating cash flow. Evidence supports coexistence of lower revenue and a larger gross-profit difference, not a claim that efficiency explains all cost reductions. [1]

Conversely, if logistics efficiency improves and feeds through to prices, it could reduce shippers’ costs and affect orders and production. Exiting businesses could also change available services and customers’ choices. These are mechanisms to test, not established macroeconomic effects. Next, watch comparable segment revenue and costs, procurement prices and expenses. The separate contributions of freight rates, business exits and efficiency remain unknown. For a water-transport engineer, read revenue, costs, gross profit and net profit in sequence; one throughput table cannot replace the full evidence chain.
